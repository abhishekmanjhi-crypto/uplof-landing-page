Most businesses I meet have already solved the hard part. They have a website,
some rankings, a form and a WhatsApp number. Nobody ever joined them up, and the
gap between them is where the enquiries go.

## What can actually be broken

An enquiry passes through five handovers before it reaches you. Any one of them
can drop it silently, and four of the five leave no trace in your analytics.

![The five handovers a lead passes through, from search to follow-up](lead-journey-ladder.png)

## How to diagnose it in twenty minutes

Do these in order and stop at the first one that fails.

1. **Search for what you sell**, on your phone, not your laptop. Does your site
   appear? If not, the problem is upstream of everything else here.
2. **Open your own site on mobile data**, not wifi. Count the seconds before you
   can read something.
3. **Fill in your own form** with a real email. Did anything arrive? Did the
   visitor get a confirmation?
4. **Message your own WhatsApp number** from a number nobody recognises. How
   long until someone replies?
5. **Look at the last ten enquiries.** Can you say what happened to each one?

## What we see most often

Step three fails more than the other four combined, and nobody notices, because
a broken form looks identical to a quiet week.

The second most common is step five. The enquiry arrived, somebody read it on
their phone, meant to reply after lunch, and it moved up the inbox. There is no
system failure to find — just no record that it was ever anyone's job. That is a
follow-up problem, not a marketing one, and it is the cheapest of the five to
fix — see the [other posts](/blog/) for how we usually close it.

## When you do not need a new website

If your site loads, says what you do, and has a working way to contact you, a
rebuild will not fix a lead problem. It will cost three months and the same
leads will go missing at the same handover.

Rebuild when the structure itself is the blocker: when there is no way to add a
page, when it cannot be edited without a developer, or when it fails on a phone.
Not because it looks dated.

## What I would fix first

Whichever handover fails earliest. There is no point improving a landing page if
the form underneath it has been broken for six weeks — you would be sending more
people into the same hole.
