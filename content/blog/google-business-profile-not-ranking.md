Almost every answer to this question online is a list of twenty tips. Google
publishes the actual factors, and there are three.

## Relevance, distance, prominence

**Relevance** is how well your profile matches what was searched. This is the
one most within your control and the one most often left half-done.

**Distance** is how far your business is from the person searching. You cannot
influence it, you cannot pay for it, and no agency can move you closer to
someone. This is the honest reason a business in one suburb struggles to appear
for searches in another.

**Prominence** is how well known you are — reviews, mentions, links, the general
weight of evidence that you exist and matter.

If you take nothing else: a ranking problem is nearly always relevance or
prominence, because distance is fixed.

## Start with the category, because it is the biggest lever

The annual local ranking surveys consistently put the primary category at or
near the top of what moves a listing in the local pack.

Most businesses pick something approximately right on day one and never look
again. Approximately right is the problem — Google matches searches to
categories quite literally.

Spend half an hour on it rather than three minutes. Look at what the businesses
ranking above you have set. Change the primary category if it is wrong, and add
the others as secondary. Do not add categories for work you cannot deliver: it
produces enquiries you have to turn down and reviews you will not like.

## Then finish the profile

Completeness is a trust signal and it is free. Most competitors do about half.

Services, each with a description. Hours you will actually answer. A proper
description written for a human. At least ten real photos. Attributes. Opening
date. A booking link if you have one.

Photos are worth a specific note: original ones. Stock images are obvious and
add nothing.

## Then reviews, which are the slow compounding one

Review signals are a meaningful share of local ranking, and close to all of
whether a stranger calls you rather than the listing above.

Going from zero to five matters far more than twenty to twenty-five. Below about
five you look untested, whatever your work is like.

Three things that matter more than volume: ask at the moment the client is
happiest rather than when you send the invoice, send the short link directly
instead of making them search, and reply to every single one. A thoughtful reply
to a critical review sells better than a five-star, because it shows a stranger
what happens when something goes wrong.

Never incentivise a review. It breaches Google's policy and risks the profile
entirely.

## Things sold to you that do nothing

**Geotagging your photos.** Google strips EXIF data on upload. Original,
relevant photos help. Their metadata does not.

**Keyword-stuffing the business name.** "Uplof Digital Marketing SEO Agency
Mumbai" is a guideline violation. Competitors report it, and profiles get edited
or suspended.

**A second profile for another city.** Duplicates get both suspended. One
profile, service areas.

**Twenty service areas.** No ranking benefit, and an implausible radius looks
like exactly what it is.

**Paying for local pack position.** Not a product. Ads are a separate, labelled
slot.

## What I would fix first

In this order, and stop when you run out of time: category, then services, then
the first five reviews.

Almost nobody gets past the first two, which is why the first two are usually
enough to move.
