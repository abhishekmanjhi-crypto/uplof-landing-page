Every business I speak to in Mumbai says a version of the same thing. The leads
are coming. They are just not sure what happened to them.

Usually they arrive in WhatsApp, which everyone treats as the problem. It is
not. WhatsApp is where your customers already are, and pulling them into a form
because it is tidier for you is a good way to lose them entirely.

## What is actually broken

A WhatsApp enquiry has none of the three things a lead needs.

**No owner.** In a shared number, everybody can see it and nobody is
responsible. In a personal number, one person is responsible and nobody else
can help when they are busy.

**No status.** A chat is either read or unread. There is no difference between
"quoted, waiting" and "never replied", and after forty messages you cannot tell
which is which.

**No reminder.** The follow-up exists only in somebody's head. It survives until
lunch.

That is the whole problem. Nothing about it is technical.

## How to check whether you have it

Open WhatsApp right now and scroll back thirty days. For each enquiry, answer:
who replied, what was the last thing said, and what is supposed to happen next.

Most people cannot answer the third one for more than about half.

If that number is uncomfortable, it is worth knowing it is the same everywhere.
It looks like a discipline problem and it is really a missing-system problem.

## The three fixes, in order

### 1. Label every new enquiry

WhatsApp Business has labels, free. Four is enough: New, Quoted, Waiting,
Closed. Nothing else.

The point is not organisation. It is that "Waiting" becomes a list you can look
at, and a list you can look at is a list you can work.

### 2. One named owner per conversation

Even alone, write it down, because you will eventually not be alone and the
habit is the hard part.

The rule that matters: an enquiry with two owners has none.

### 3. One reminder per open conversation

Not a follow-up system. One reminder, on the day you decide to follow up. Phone
alarm is fine.

Most deals are lost to silence rather than to a no, and the second message is
where a surprising number of them turn.

## What we see most often

The follow-up that never happened is almost never a decision. Nobody chose to
ignore a customer. Somebody read the message on the way to something else,
meant to reply properly later, and later the chat had moved up the list.

The second most common thing: businesses that already fixed this for phone calls
and email, and never thought to extend it to WhatsApp, because WhatsApp feels
personal rather than operational.

## When you need a CRM, and when you do not

If you are one or two people and the three fixes above are in place, you
probably do not need one yet. Labels and a reminder will carry you a long way,
and a CRM nobody updates is worse than no CRM.

You need one when you cannot answer "what happened to that enquiry from
Tuesday" without scrolling, when more than two people touch a lead, or when you
want to know which marketing actually produced a customer rather than which
produced a click. That last one is impossible in WhatsApp alone, and it is
usually the reason people finally move.

If you are at that point, [we can trace where your enquiries are
going](/contact/) before you buy anything.

## The India-specific part

Two things make this different here.

Click-to-WhatsApp is often the highest-converting button on an Indian site —
frequently more than a form. So the answer is never "stop using WhatsApp".

And a `wa.me` link carries no tracking into the conversation. The chat opens and
the source is gone. If you want to know which post or which ad produced a
customer, either use a different pre-filled message per channel so the text
itself identifies the source, or send people to a tagged page that has the
WhatsApp button on it. There is more on that in the [rest of the
insights](/blog/).

## What I would fix first

Labels. Today, in ten minutes. Then look at the Waiting list at the end of each
day for a week and see how many were sitting there without you knowing.

That number is usually the whole argument for doing the other two.
