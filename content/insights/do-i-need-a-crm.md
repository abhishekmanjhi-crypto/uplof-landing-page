This question usually arrives after somebody has been sold one. So here is the
version that is not trying to sell you anything.

## Four questions

Answer honestly. They take a minute.

1. Can you say what happened to every enquiry from the last thirty days, without
   scrolling through anything?
2. Does more than one person touch a lead between first contact and payment?
3. Do you know which marketing produced your last five customers — not which
   produced clicks, which produced customers?
4. Has anyone followed up with you personally about a lead you forgot?

**Mostly no to 2, 3 and 4, and yes to 1** — you do not need a CRM. Fix the
follow-up habit instead.

**Yes to 2** — you need something shared, and it does not have to be a CRM yet.

**No to 3** — this is the one that usually justifies it. Attribution is
essentially impossible without a place where the lead and its source live
together.

**Yes to 4** — you already know.

## What a CRM actually does

Strip away the feature lists and it does three things.

**It gives a lead a status.** Not read or unread. New, quoted, waiting,
won, lost. So "what is outstanding" is a question with an answer.

**It gives a lead an owner.** One name. A lead owned by everyone is owned by
nobody.

**It remembers.** So following up does not depend on anyone's memory, which is
the part that fails first when you get busy — exactly when it costs most.

Everything else is decoration until those three are working.

## Why most small-business CRMs fail

Not the software. The set-up.

Somebody imports every contact they have ever had, builds nine pipeline stages,
adds twenty custom fields, and then nobody fills any of them in. Three weeks
later the CRM is out of date, which makes it worse than useless — now you have
a system that actively lies to you.

If you do get one, start with four stages and five fields. Add more only when
something is genuinely missing. It is much easier to add a field than to get
people to fill in one they do not believe in.

## If you are in India, this matters more than the feature list

Your leads are in WhatsApp. A CRM that cannot see WhatsApp will get updated for
the first fortnight and then quietly abandoned, because keeping two systems in
sync by hand is work nobody sustains.

Check WhatsApp integration before you check anything else. It is worth more than
any feature comparison, and it is the thing most international tools treat as an
add-on.

Worth knowing: some popular free tiers exclude WhatsApp messaging and put it
behind a paid marketing plan. That turns a free tool into a monthly cost for the
one feature you actually needed.

## What I would do first, if the answer is "not yet"

Do the three things in the [WhatsApp
piece](/insights/whatsapp-leads-not-followed-up/) — label, owner, reminder. They
take an afternoon, cost nothing, and give you most of what a CRM would.

Then run this test after a month: can you say what happened to every enquiry?
If yes, you still do not need one. If no, you now know exactly which of the
three broke, and that tells you what to look for when you do buy something.

## When it is genuinely worth doing properly

When the cost of a forgotten lead is bigger than the cost of the system. For
most service businesses, one lost job a month pays for any of these tools
several times over — which sounds like an argument for buying one, and is
really an argument for knowing your numbers first.
